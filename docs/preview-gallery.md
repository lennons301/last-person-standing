# Component preview gallery

`/preview` renders components against hand-built fixtures — no auth, no database, no live game. It's the review surface for UI work. The rules for keeping a gallery that way (route gating, where fixtures live, no database, client rows in sibling files) are in `AGENTS.md` under "Component preview gallery"; this file records, route by route, which states each gallery covers and why those are the states worth reviewing. When you add a state to a state-driven component, add its fixture to the gallery and its entry here in the same PR.

## `/preview/game-hero`

`/preview/game-hero` covers the game page's top-of-page chrome — the identity bar, the stat line (including the pot disclosure and the unpaid notice) and every `GameHero` state × game mode — from descriptors shaped like `buildGameView`'s output.

## `/preview/live-scores`

`/preview/live-scores` covers the on-demand live scores pop-out from hand-built live payloads (including live-window scenarios a database won't produce on demand, and the three states a pick's **pre-match win chance** puts a card in: priced picks in play — the 22% shot leading and the 84% favourite held — a competition carrying no prices at all, and a round with a price on one of the viewer's picks and none on the other, which is where "absent renders as nothing, never 0%" is reviewed; the payload carries two viewer picks in those sections because a rebuy leaves the viewer two player rows).

## `/preview/picks`

`/preview/picks` covers the pick selector's shared `FixtureRow` — the nested planner context and the form-detail panel included. It is grouped: a shared-row section (the states any mode can put the row in, including a fixture with bookmaker win-probabilities and the same fixture unpriced), then one section per mode. Classic's group also carries the **Table view** (`PickTable`): the same round as a standings board, one row per pickable team, at full width and at both phone widths (375px and 360px — the board fits five columns with no horizontal scroll, so the narrow columns are where that is measured), in the states the board has to survive — fully priced, the phone worst case (a used-team chip under one name and a team with no form beside it), unpriced, partly priced (the case the sink-to-the-bottom sort rule exists for), a season start with nothing played, and used/restricted teams. Classic's sections carry the full `SideState` matrix in classic's own wording, and `ClassicPick` itself in each state the card moves through (no pick / selected / locked-collapsed / round-closed) — the card states are what make the hero de-duplication reviewable, since what matters there is the round name and deadline being *absent*. Cup's section is `CupPick` itself (nothing ranked / part-ranked / read-only), reviewing the same absence plus what cup gets from the shared row for nothing: it sources neither form nor league position — a cup team's form lives in its league, deferred to the FA-Cup effort — so the bottom bar stands down and the tier annotations carry the row. Turbo's sections cover its whole picker — the real `TurboPick`, in each state of its submission (nothing ranked / partially ranked / fully ranked / unsaved changes / season start with no form / opened on the Table view) — plus its ranked rows in isolation and the **Table view as a ranking board**: the same `PickTable` in the mode where a tap adds to the confidence set, at all three widths (its sixth column makes it the tightest the board gets), in the states that mode adds (nothing ranked, part-ranked including a draw call that marks both of a fixture's rows, ranked to the round's count, unpriced, read-only). A closing form-detail section covers the enriched tap-through sheet (`TeamFormPanel`) at both widths: the home/away venue split with goals for and against, and the full home/draw/away market — which arrives with the row rather than from the form query, so it renders while the form is still loading and survives a form failure, and is absent entirely for an unpriced fixture.

## `/preview/form-guide`

`/preview/form-guide` covers the full form-guide page (`FormGuideView`) the sheet leads on to, in the five states the data can put it in: mid-season from a pick (position line, home/away splits, priced next fixture, head-to-head), opened from a badge with no opponent (no head-to-head section at all), early season (a two-point line, an unpriced next fixture, two teams who haven't met), opening weekend before this team has kicked off (a real position against the full table — "14th of 20", the state the position-line snapshot ships into on day one — with no line yet), and season start with nothing recorded — the state that proves each section says what it doesn't know rather than rendering blank.

## `/preview/me-summary`

`/preview/me-summary` covers the player's own summary page (`PlayerSummaryView`) at full width and at 375px, in four states: a full history (the career headline, then all three mode sections populated — classic's rounds survived and its round one, which is where the rebought state is reviewed (two opening picks down, both of them in games that offered a rebuy, one of the two bought back into — so the rebuy figure is read against those two rather than against all four classic games, and the one that wasn't holds picks in later rounds all the same, advance picks locked in before the exit that the elimination round is what tells apart from a rebuy); turbo's and cup's streaks, each with its competition rows — and the Teams section closing the page), the same history arrived at through a filtering link (the league block narrowed to one of its two seasons and still offering the other, the World Cup block narrowed to an edition this player never played so it names the empty season instead of showing noughts, and everything above the Teams section unmoved), and a classic-only player whose one turbo game is still being played (cup states its own absence; turbo has a record but no completed game, so it has no streak to average yet, and classic's round one has never gone down, so its rebuy figure says none was ever on offer rather than showing a nought that would read as a chance passed up), and a player one round into a game created mid-season, whose opening round — gameweek 12, that game's round one — hasn't kicked off (nothing has settled, so the survival rate is a dash over a game it has nothing to say about yet rather than a nought for a hurdle the player hasn't been put to, and the accuracy rate beside it says the same of the pending pick; a round one a cancelled fixture voided renders identically). All four fixtures are built by running hand-written rows through the real `buildMeSummaryView` rather than hand-totalling a `MeSummaryView` — hand-built (no database) but never hand-*summed*, because the states worth reviewing here are relationships between figures (two Premier League seasons pooling into one block while the World Cup stands apart, a rate that excludes the picks a life absorbed, a team with no rate in neither end, a streak absent because its game has not completed) and a hand-totalled model can assert those without them being true. A closing money section reviews the fold both ways: `MoneySection` on its own (shut, the state a player lands on), then `MoneyPanel` rendered straight at full width and at 375px, which is how the *opened* state is reviewed without a click — the whole reason the presentational half is split from the fold state. Its fixture adds payment and payout rows to the same history (a rebuy as a second row, an entry only marked paid, a wiped-out game refunded to nothing, a free game with no rows at all, two shared payouts) so the headline is the loss most players are looking at, and a second panel covers a player who has only ever played for nothing. A final section covers the page's **Settings fold** — as it renders on the page (collapsed), then opened in both states the setting has: a handle saved and none saved at all, plus one at 375px. The fold takes a `defaultOpen` only so the gallery can show what's behind the toggle; the page never passes it.

## `/preview/discover`

`/preview/discover` covers the home page's public-game half: `DiscoverGameCard` in both states (open to join and in progress) at full width and at 375px, then each section — the ordered open list, including the pre-draw knockout game whose round carries no deadline and so sorts last with its date to be confirmed rather than first as an unknown, and the collapsed in-progress list — and finally both sections handed an empty list, which is how "renders nothing at all, no empty header" is reviewed. Its fixture is hand-written rows run through the real `buildDiscoverView` (same reason as `/preview/me-summary`: the states worth reviewing are which games appear and in what order, and a hand-split `DiscoverView` could assert either without it being true), and it deliberately feeds in the three kinds of game that must never be listed — private, one the viewer is already in, and completed — so the gallery shows them being dropped rather than merely omitting them.

## `/preview/round-summary`

`/preview/round-summary` covers the post-deadline round summary card (`RoundSummaryCard`) at full width and at 360px — the width where price-plus-probability on one line is tightest — first as a player meets it (collapsed, the most-backed line in its trigger), then opened in each of the eight states the field's picks put it in: a canonical priced round, an unpriced competition (three tiles gone, one line saying why), a round nobody backed an underdog in, a round with no contested fixture (Head to head absent entirely), the whole field on one team, a round containing auto-picks (counted everywhere but the boldest calls, marked `(auto)`), a round a player made no pick in, and a two-player field. The card takes a `defaultOpen` only so the gallery can show what's behind the fold; the page never passes it. Each section prints the share text for the same state beneath the card, which is how the prose is reviewed without a clipboard. Its fixtures are hand-written pick rows run through the real `buildRoundSummary` (same reason as `/preview/discover`: the states worth reviewing are relationships between figures — a tile absent because a round carries no prices, an auto-pick in every tile but one, a clash the field sits on both sides of — and a hand-assembled `RoundSummaryView` could assert all of them without any being true).
